*How a search for a family home became a vision to protect natural springs for generations.*

In 2022, I purchased a piece of land with a simple dream: to build a home where I could settle with my family.

At the time, I had no intention of starting a bottled-water company or building a conservation enterprise. I was simply looking for a place my family could call home.

As fate would have it, the land I purchased was adjacent to a natural spring.

What struck me was how important that spring had become to the surrounding community, particularly the growing population around a nearby university.

Seeing how many people depended on this natural water source made me realise that more needed to be done to protect it.

**What began as a personal investment in my family's future would eventually become a commitment to protecting a natural resource for generations to come.**

## A Decision to Leave the Land Undisturbed

After acquiring the land, I made a personal decision: because it was adjacent to the spring, I would not use it for farming.

Rather than cultivate the land, I left it open and allowed boys from the village to use it as a football field.

As the nearby university continued to attract people, I also faced pressure to develop rental houses on the property. The financial opportunity was tempting, but I was concerned about the potential impact of development on the spring's surrounding environment.

Conservation has always been close to my heart, so I resisted that pressure.

The land remained undeveloped.

## The Question That Changed Everything

For some time, I was content with my decision. I had acquired the land, avoided farming, resisted commercial development, and done what I believed was right for the spring.

But one day, a question crossed my mind that completely changed how I viewed what I was doing.

**What happens when I am no longer here?**

I realised that my approach to conservation depended heavily on my personal convictions.

I could protect the land during my lifetime, but what guarantee did I have that the next generation would share the same conservation mindset?

Would they resist the same financial pressures? Would they make the same decisions?

That was when I understood that personal commitment alone was not enough.

**A conservation effort that depends entirely on one person's goodwill may not survive that person.**

I needed to find a more sustainable approach.

## From Personal Conviction to a Sustainable Model

I began asking myself whether conservation could be supported by a business model that would make protecting the spring financially sustainable.

What if the spring could support a responsible commercial enterprise, and that enterprise could, in turn, help finance the spring's long-term protection?

What if environmental conservation could become part of the business itself rather than depending entirely on the personal beliefs of whoever owned the land?

And what if that approach could eventually extend to other natural springs?

Those questions gave rise to the idea of **Watercana**.

I envisioned a business that would responsibly produce and distribute spring water while committing part of its profits to protecting natural springs, restoring their surrounding environments, and supporting conservation awareness in local communities.

A business where commercial success and environmental protection would reinforce each other.

Most importantly, I wanted to build something that could continue long after I was gone.

## Conservation With Every Sip

Today, Watercana is being developed around a straightforward principle: the natural water sources that sustain a business should also benefit from its success.

Our model involves protecting land adjacent to natural springs, supporting indigenous reforestation, establishing responsible water production, and committing **20% of future net profits** to spring conservation and community environmental awareness.

We are still at the beginning of this journey.

Our first spring is in Belgut, Kenya, where we have acquired adjacent land and undertaken initial conservation activities. We have also begun assessing the water source as we prepare for further testing and the development of a suitable processing facility.

Commercial bottling has not yet started.

But the vision extends far beyond our first spring.

My hope is that Watercana will eventually help protect other natural springs through a model that can sustain itself commercially and endure across generations.

## A Legacy Beyond Ownership

When I purchased that land in 2022, I was thinking about providing a home for my family.

I never imagined that the decision would lead me to question how conservation could survive beyond an individual's lifetime.

Looking back, I realise that acquiring the land was only the beginning.

The bigger challenge was finding a way to ensure that the spring could remain protected regardless of who owned the land in the future.

I don't know how far Watercana will go. Like any new venture, there will be challenges, lessons, and difficult decisions along the way.

But I know what I want it to stand for.

I want Watercana to demonstrate that conservation does not have to depend entirely on donations, goodwill, or the personal convictions of one generation.

It can be built into a commercially sustainable model that gives future generations a reason, and the means, to continue protecting the natural resources they inherit.

**What began as a search for a family home has become a vision for something much bigger: protecting natural springs for generations to come.**

And that is the story behind Watercana.

*Conservation with every sip.*
